# Harbourline Foods: Cold-Chain Consolidation Assessment

Prepared by the Network Planning Group for the Operations Committee. Status: draft 3, circulated for decision. Synthetic document created for testing; all names and figures are invented.

## 1. Purpose and scope

Harbourline Foods operates five regional cold-storage depots (Aldwick, Brenmoor, Calder Quay, Dunmere and Eastgate) serving 412 supermarket and food-service customers. This assessment evaluates whether to consolidate the five depots into three by the end of fiscal year 2028 (FY28), closing Brenmoor and Dunmere and expanding Calder Quay. It covers cost, service levels, food-safety risk, workforce impact and delivery risk. It does not cover the separate decision on replacing the refrigerant plant at Aldwick, which is tracked under the Capital Plan and is assumed to proceed in either scenario.

The Committee is asked to choose one of three options at its March meeting: Option A (status quo with targeted upgrades), Option B (consolidate to three depots, phased over 24 months) or Option C (consolidate to four depots by closing Dunmere only). The Planning Group recommends Option B, subject to the conditions in section 8.

## 2. Current network performance

The five depots handled 61,400 pallet movements per week on average in FY26, against a combined design capacity of 84,000. Utilisation varies widely. Aldwick ran at 88 percent of capacity and Calder Quay at 81 percent, while Brenmoor ran at 49 percent and Dunmere at 54 percent. Eastgate ran at 71 percent.

Fixed costs per depot are dominated by refrigeration energy, which accounts for 38 percent of depot operating cost, followed by labour at 34 percent and property costs at 17 percent. The remaining 11 percent is maintenance, insurance and systems. Total network operating cost in FY26 was 118.6 million, of which Brenmoor accounted for 17.9 million and Dunmere for 19.4 million.

On-time-in-full (OTIF) delivery performance averaged 94.2 percent across the network in FY26. The contractual floor with the three largest customers is 93.0 percent, with service credits of 1.5 percent of invoice value for each full percentage point below the floor. Brenmoor posted the best OTIF at 96.1 percent, which the depot managers attribute to its short average drop distance of 38 kilometres. Calder Quay posted the worst at 91.8 percent, driven by dock congestion during the two evening peaks.

Temperature excursions, defined as any recorded product temperature outside the agreed band for more than 20 minutes, occurred 143 times network-wide in FY26. Of these, 61 occurred in transit and 82 on site. Forty-one site excursions were traced to the ageing compressors at Dunmere, and this is the single largest identified cause. The excursions led to product write-offs of 2.3 million in total.

## 3. Option B: consolidation to three depots

Under Option B, Brenmoor would close at the end of month 12 and Dunmere at the end of month 24. Calder Quay would be expanded from 21,000 to 29,500 pallet positions, adding a second chilled dock and a mezzanine for order picking. Aldwick and Eastgate would be unchanged apart from routine maintenance.

The pro-forma places the post-consolidation network at 86 percent average utilisation, assuming volumes grow at the FY26 to FY28 forecast of 2.1 percent a year and that 70 percent of Brenmoor and Dunmere volume transfers to Calder Quay with the remainder going to Aldwick and Eastgate. The Planning Group regards 86 percent as the upper end of what the network can sustain without service degradation; above 90 percent, the pilot at Aldwick in FY25 showed OTIF falling by roughly 2.5 points.

Steady-state annual savings are estimated at 4.1 million, built from three components: energy (2.2 million), property (1.3 million) and labour (0.6 million). The labour saving is small because 60 percent of affected roles are expected to transfer to Calder Quay or Aldwick rather than leave the business. Redundancy and transfer costs are included in the one-off figures below, not in the steady-state saving.

One-off costs for Option B are 21.5 million: construction at Calder Quay (12.8 million), redundancy and relocation (4.9 million), decommissioning and site restoration (2.6 million) and programme management (1.2 million). On these figures simple payback is 5.2 years from the start of steady state. The Planning Group notes that the discount rate used in the model is the corporate hurdle rate of 9 percent, and that on this basis the net present value over ten years is a positive 3.4 million, but it falls to negative territory if savings fall short by more than 11 percent.

Please see the correction in Appendix B, which supersedes the steady-state savings figure given in this section.

## 4. Options A and C

Option A keeps all five depots and spends 9.6 million over three years on targeted upgrades: replacing the Dunmere compressors (4.1 million), a dock scheduling system at Calder Quay (1.4 million), and energy-efficiency measures across the network (4.1 million). The Planning Group estimates the energy measures would save 1.1 million a year. Option A does not address the underutilisation at Brenmoor and Dunmere, which together run roughly 4,300 pallet positions of idle capacity a week, but it carries the lowest delivery risk and no workforce impact.

Option C closes Dunmere only, at the end of month 18, and moves its volume to Calder Quay and Eastgate. One-off costs are 11.2 million and steady-state savings 2.0 million a year, giving simple payback of 5.6 years. Option C keeps Brenmoor open, preserving the best OTIF performer, but leaves the network at 77 percent utilisation, which the Planning Group considers inefficient. Under Option C the Dunmere compressor replacement would not be needed, and that avoided cost of 4.1 million has already been netted from the one-off figure.

## 5. Service-level and food-safety risk

The main service risk in Option B is Calder Quay. It already has the worst OTIF in the network, and the expansion would raise its throughput by around 40 percent. The proposed second dock and an electronic dock-scheduling system are intended to remove the evening congestion. The Planning Group modelled the effect and expects Calder Quay OTIF to improve to 94 percent after the expansion, but this relies on the scheduling system being live before the first volume transfer. Without it, the model projects network OTIF falling to 92.6 percent, which would put the three largest contracts below the 93.0 percent floor and trigger service credits estimated at 1.9 million a year.

Longer drop distances are a second risk. Moving Brenmoor volume to Calder Quay would increase the average drop distance for the affected customers from 38 to 71 kilometres. Transit excursions are correlated with journey time; the Planning Group's regression suggests each additional 10 kilometres adds 0.4 transit excursions per thousand deliveries. This is a modest effect, and it is partly offset by the removal of the Dunmere compressors from service, which eliminates the largest identified source of site excursions.

During the transition there is a short period of elevated food-safety risk. Both transferring depots will be operated at reduced staffing for roughly four months before closure, and inventory will be moved in a compressed window. The Planning Group proposes a dedicated temperature-monitoring regime with loggers on every transferred pallet. The cost of the loggers, 0.35 million, is included in programme management.

## 6. Workforce impact

Brenmoor employs 212 people and Dunmere 247, a combined 459. The Planning Group assumes 60 percent of roles transfer, 25 percent are made redundant and 15 percent leave voluntarily before closure. This implies about 115 redundancies. Consultation with the works council must begin at least 90 days before any redundancy announcement, and the works council has indicated informally that it will oppose closure of Brenmoor, where the average length of service is 14 years.

Transfers to Calder Quay depend on the expansion being complete. If construction slips, transferring staff would have to be accommodated at reduced productivity, or retained at Brenmoor beyond month 12 at a cost of approximately 0.4 million per month. The Planning Group considers a six-month slip plausible, based on the delay history of the two previous capital projects at Calder Quay (a five-month delay and a seven-month delay).

## 7. Delivery risk and dependencies

Three dependencies drive the schedule. First, planning permission for the mezzanine at Calder Quay, which the local authority has indicated will take between 4 and 9 months. Second, the dock-scheduling system, which the vendor can deliver in 7 months from contract. Third, the works council consultation described above. Option B has a critical path of 22 months to the first volume transfer if all three run in parallel and nothing slips, leaving only a two-month margin against the month-24 closure of Dunmere.

The Planning Group has assessed the probability of completing Option B on schedule at 45 percent and the probability of completing within six months of schedule at 80 percent. It has not assessed the probability of a materially different outcome, such as the planning application being refused. A refusal would leave the network in the worst of all positions, with Brenmoor closed and insufficient capacity at Calder Quay; the Planning Group therefore proposes making Brenmoor's closure conditional on planning consent.

## 8. Recommendation and conditions

The Planning Group recommends Option B, on five conditions: (1) planning consent for the Calder Quay mezzanine is secured before the Brenmoor closure date is confirmed; (2) the dock-scheduling system is live at least one month before the first volume transfer; (3) the works council consultation starts no later than month 3; (4) a contingency of 2.0 million is added to the one-off budget, bringing the total request to 23.5 million; and (5) the Committee reviews progress at month 9 with the option to stop and revert to Option C.

If the Committee prefers lower delivery risk, the Planning Group's second choice is Option C, followed by Option A. The Planning Group does not recommend Option A on its own, since the idle capacity at Brenmoor and Dunmere would persist and the Dunmere compressors would still need replacing within 36 months.

The decision requested is approval of the 23.5 million capital request for Option B, and authority to open works council consultation. If the Committee decides to approve, the first required action is to begin the planning application, which must be submitted by the end of this quarter to keep the critical path intact.

## Appendix A: Key assumptions

Volume growth of 2.1 percent a year. Transfer share of 70 percent from closed depots to Calder Quay. Discount rate of 9 percent. Energy price flat in real terms; a 15 percent rise would add 0.9 million to Option B's annual savings but also raise Option A's costs. Labour inflation of 3.5 percent a year. Customer volumes assumed retained at 100 percent; no allowance has been made for customers leaving in response to disruption, and the commercial team has said it cannot rule out the loss of one of the three largest customers, which represent 38 percent of revenue.

## Appendix B: Correction notice

After circulation of draft 2, Finance found that the property saving in section 3 double counted the lease break at Dunmere. The corrected property saving is 0.4 million, not 1.3 million. The steady-state annual saving for Option B is therefore 3.2 million, not 4.1 million, and simple payback is 6.7 years. The net present value over ten years on the corrected figures is negative 3.1 million. The recommendation in section 8 was written before this correction and has not been revisited. Savings for Options A and C are unaffected.
